WavePhaseNet in 3 minutes

Hallucination isn't a training gap.
It follows from the shape of the space.

WavePhaseNet reframes LLM hallucination as something that must happen given the geometry of the representation space — not something more data will fix — and proposes suppressing it by using the Fourier transform to build a semantic conceptual hierarchy directly inside the embedding space.

Kiyotaka Kasubuchi & Kazuo Fukiya / January 12, 2026 / arXiv:2602.14419
SCROLL
01 // ISSUE

Why more data does not remove hallucination

The usual diagnosis is "not enough training data" or "bad prompting". This paper starts from the claim that neither explains what we observe.

📊

Optimized for average plausibility

What an LLM optimizes is average quality over a probability distribution — never the truth of any individual claim.

🧩

Locally sound, globally contradictory

Each paragraph holds up, yet the first half contradicts the second. Nothing enforces consistency between overlapping local contexts.

🎚️

Dependence on prompt craftsmanship

Complex reasoning demands sophisticated prompt design from the user, which makes results person-dependent and hard to scale.

Root cause: the shapes do not match

Here is the core claim: the set of true statements and the space an LLM can represent have fundamentally different shapes.

The truth set

  • Jagged, with disconnected islands (non-convex)
  • Blending two true claims does not yield a true claim
  • Averaging "A is profitable" and "B is in the red" produces nothing meaningful

The representation space

  • Smooth and gap-free (linear and convex)
  • A midpoint between any two points always exists
  • That midpoint becomes a claim that does not exist in reality

Approximating a jagged set of truths with a smooth space. As long as that mismatch of shape persists, more training will keep producing statements that never existed. So the paper proposes rebuilding the space, not enlarging the data.

02 // IDEA

Decompose text as a wave

WavePhaseNet uses three tools. All are mature signal-processing techniques; what is new is applying them to meaning.

01
DISCRETE FOURIER TRANSFORM

Frequency decomposition = an equalizer for meaning

Instead of a sequence of words, the text is viewed as a superposition of waves. Slowly varying components (low frequency) carry the theme and intent of the whole document; rapidly varying ones (high frequency) carry word order and phrasing.

Think of an audio equalizer. Raise the bass and the foundation comes forward; raise the treble and the detail does. Text has the same band structure, and the DFT hands you the knobs.
02
SEMANTIC CONCEPTUAL HIERARCHY STRUCTURE

SCHS = carve the hierarchy into the space itself

External dictionaries — thesauri, ontologies — cannot be embedded directly into the representation space, and RAG brings heavy external dependency with poor extensibility. So the frequency bands themselves become the hierarchy, putting semantic levels inside the space.

The intended payoff is escaping prompt craftsmanship. If the hierarchy lives in the space, the model can track which level of abstraction it is operating at without the user writing elaborate instructions.
03
COHOMOLOGY REGULARIZATION

Cohomology regularization = cross-checking testimony

The document is split into overlapping windows, each producing its own interpretation. Where windows overlap and interpretations disagree, that disagreement is measured as a loss and reduced during training. The component on which every window agrees can be extracted mathematically.

Think of cross-checking eyewitness accounts. Where accounts overlap and conflict, something is wrong. Extracting the part where every account agrees — the harmonic component — is what Hodge theory does.
03 // HOW

How one document flows through it

1
Embed
Turn the token sequence into an embedding matrix (length N × dimension d)
2
FFT into the frequency domain
Transform along the sequence axis — cheap, near-linear in sequence length
3
Separate the bands
Split into low (intent), mid (discourse structure) and high (phrasing) frequencies
4
Fix the low band as the intent vector
Extract the direction the document is heading and keep referring to it throughout generation
5
Generate the high band conditioned on that intent
Phrasing and word order are produced only within what the fixed intent allows
6
Transform back
The inverse DFT restores the original form — reconstruction is exact, nothing is lost
7
Penalize disagreement between windows
Add the contradiction between overlapping windows to the loss and train it down

Importantly, this does not replace your Transformer. It is implemented as a spectral module and a regularizer injected per layer and per head, with the low-frequency intent added to the attention input as a residual.

What is being claimed

Natural language follows Zipf's law and is self-similar, so embedding energy concentrates at low frequencies and decays as 1/f. Accumulating from the bottom up, roughly 3,000 dimensions capture 82–88% of the total. That, the paper argues, is the lower bound at which meaning and intent survive.

Below it, the claim goes, semantically similar sentences become indistinguishable from merely syntactically similar ones. Turned around: most of the 24,576 dimensions are redundant, and rigorous reasoning remains possible at one eighth the size.

⚠ Do not misread the status of these numbers
  • 82–88% is derived from the 1/f model, not measured. It is not the result of analyzing actual GPT-4 embeddings.
  • The paper contains no evaluation, no benchmark comparison and no ablation study. What it offers is a framework and a validation plan.
  • So nobody can yet say "deploy this and hallucination drops by X%". Validation on real data is named as the next step.
  • The successor work, QuantumPhaseNet, rewrites the same idea and does carry prototype measurements.
05 // WHY FOURIER

"Just use a Fourier transform" is not the point

Replacing attention with a Fourier transform has been done before (FNet). So what is different here? This is the dividing line.

What FNet does

  • Replaces attention with a Fourier transform for speed
  • Discards the imaginary part of the complex representation
  • Consequently handles coreference and context dependency poorly

What WavePhaseNet does

  • Transforms for semantic manipulation, not speed
  • Preserves phase — hence "Phase" in the name
  • Inserts semantic operators per band to manipulate the hierarchy explicitly

An invertible transform has no value by itself. The value is in what you do once you are on the other side. Move to the frequency domain, reweight the bands, move back — the operation sandwiched in the middle is the method. "Keep the global topic, attenuate phrasing, remove this contrastive structure" becomes something you can write down rigorously in linear algebra.

06 // USE CASES

Where it earns its keep

The payoff concentrates where you must hold a long document to a single thread.

Long-form report generation

Investigation and technical reports. Fixing the low-frequency intent addresses drift away from the original brief.

→ low frequency = intent, referenced throughout

Cross-checking multiple documents

Spec versus implementation, contract versus actual practice — detecting contradictions between overlapping sources.

→ cohomology regularization quantifies disagreement

Reducing inference cost

If dimensions really can drop from 24,576 to 3,000, memory and compute fall substantially.

→ justified by cumulative energy analysis

Edge and embedded inference

The domain the paper calls fatal: rigor is required and compute is scarce at the same time.

→ designed to combine low dimension with rigor

Summarization and intent extraction

The theme of a whole document is available as a single vector, usable as a front end for summarization or classification.

→ obtained directly as the global intent vector g

Escaping prompt dependency

Putting the semantic hierarchy in the space lowers reliance on the user's prompt-design skill.

→ SCHS built inside the embedding space
07 // SCOPE

What is settled, and what is not

Mathematically established

  • The DFT is invertible: the inverse transform reconstructs the embedding exactly
  • Conjugate symmetry guarantees real-valued reconstruction (only half the high band must be learned)
  • The null space of the Laplacian corresponds to the gluable, contradiction-free component
  • FFT, sparse linear algebra and iterative solvers make the computation scale

Not yet established

  • Effectiveness on real data (no benchmark evaluation was run)
  • Whether the 3,000-dimension bound holds for actual GPT-4 embeddings
  • Whether "low frequency = correct intent" holds across all documents
  • The contribution of each of λ, μ and η (no ablation study was run)
⚠ Hallucination will not go to zero
  • The paper states outright that genuine obstructions from higher cohomology remain. Projection removes only part of the problem.
  • Nothing guarantees that low frequency means correct intent. Misinformation repeated at length also becomes a low-frequency component.
  • Since the truth set is non-convex, no linear representation space can match it exactly. The goal is suppression, not elimination.
  • In high-stakes work, never let the output be the sole basis for a decision: build in human review and source attribution.
08 // ADOPTION

If you adopt it, adopt it in this order

The paper designs the implementation as an injection into a Transformer. Ordered by how cheaply each step can be tested:

stagewhat you dowhat you learn
1Measure the embedding spectrumWhether your data really shows 1/f structure
2Compute cumulative energyHow many dimensions give what percentage (is 3,000 right?)
3Reduce dimensions and compare accuracyWhether performance survives the cut
4Inject the spectral moduleWhether low-frequency intent injection helps long-form generation
5Add cohomology regularizationWhether local contradictions actually decrease (the key test)
6Ablate λ, μ and ηWhich term is doing the work

Stages 1–2 only analyze an existing model's embeddings — no model surgery required. They test the paper's central claim against your own data at minimal cost. If that fails, there is no point going further.

09 // FAQ

Frequently asked

Does this replace the LLM we already use?

No. It is designed as a spectral module and a regularizer injected per layer and per head, with the low-frequency intent added to the attention input as a residual.

We already run RAG. Does this make it unnecessary?

The paper criticizes RAG for heavy external dependency and poor extensibility, and argues for putting the semantic hierarchy inside the embedding space. That is not a claim to replace RAG — external knowledge retrieval operates at a different layer.

Does it increase compute cost?

The FFT is near-linear in sequence length and cheap. The Laplacian is sparse and handled by iterative solvers. If the dimensionality reduction holds up, total cost may actually fall.

By how much does hallucination decrease?

The paper has no experiments, so there is no quantitative answer. What it provides is the framework and the metrics to evaluate (perplexity, local consistency rate, Zipf deviation). Validation on your own data comes first.

How does this relate to QuantumPhaseNet?

WavePhaseNet is the earlier paper and QuantumPhaseNet the successor. The successor rewrites the same idea in a gauge-covariant framework, redefining phase as a frame-independent quantity, and adds prototype measurements.

Is a proposal without experiments worth anything?

The paper's main object is to formalize why hallucination is structurally unavoidable. Formalizing the cause comes first; demonstrating the remedy is left to follow-up work — which the successor paper does carry out.
