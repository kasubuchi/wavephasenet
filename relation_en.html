From WavePhaseNet to QuantumPhaseNet,
then ModularPhaseNet

Three papers by the same authors. The intuition is continuous, but the mathematical stage was swapped out twice: a continuous Fourier phase → a gauge-covariant complex geometry → a finite cyclic group that closes in integer arithmetic. What changed, what did not — and, just as importantly, what was left behind.

2026.08.11
(English 09.05)
ModularPhaseNet
Quantizes the continuous complex phase into a finite cyclic group \(G=\langle g\rangle\subset\mathbb{F}_p^{\times}\). The quantum Fourier transform, phase estimation and amplitude amplification are all removed, leaving inference to modular exponentiation and table lookups. No experiments were run; a pre-registered evaluation plan and falsification criteria are published instead.

The second move was the same intuition rebuilt on mathematics that breaks less easily. The third move has a different motive: taking that mathematics and landing it on hardware that already exists. One migration was made for correctness, the other for executability — reading them that way makes the sequence easier to follow.

02 // SHARED

Foundations that run through all three

The stage changed twice; the roots did not.

How the problem is framed

Hallucination follows from the structure of the representation space, not from insufficient training. So rebuild the space, not the dataset. Both the diagnosis and the prescription carry through all three papers.

Frequency and phase map to meaning

Slowly varying components carry the intent of a whole document; rapidly varying ones carry local expression. The third paper inherits this as "small phase change = long wavelength = higher-level concept" — and, like its predecessors, treats it as an empirical hypothesis.

Phase is never discarded

All three retain phase and use it for semantic manipulation. The third discretizes phase but does not throw it away: after rounding onto ticks, composition, inversion and relative phase are computed exactly.

The Transformer is not replaced

All are designed as correction terms and residuals; zero the coefficients and the original model returns exactly. The third goes further — every added quantity is a real-valued scalar bias, so no existing kernel has to be modified.

Proofs and hypotheses stay separate

Separating what can be proved from what can be refuted on real data became explicit in the second paper; the third organizes it further into five proved theorems, the empirical hypotheses, five claims deliberately avoided, and seven falsification criteria.

Intrinsic structure first (external evidence divides the labor)

All three build the semantic hierarchy inside the representation. Their treatment of external knowledge diverges: WavePhaseNet criticizes RAG for heavy external dependency, whereas both successors actively incorporate retrieval — as the evidence term in attention and the evidence-deficiency term in risk.

04 // CORE

Three changes are doing the real work

The table has many rows, but three changes matter most when reading the papers against each other. The first two belong to the move from the first paper to the second; the third to the move from the second to the third.

① Phase: from coordinate to invariant (Wave → Quantum)

WAVEPHASENET

Two terms are compared through the difference of their phase angles. But \(\Phi_k\) is a coordinate — it depends on the chosen basis.

Writing \(\cos(\varphi_i - \varphi_j)\) silently assumes that every term shares one common reference axis.

→
QUANTUMPHASENET

Parallel transport is inserted before the inner product, so two points can be compared without fixing any reference axis.

That the value is unchanged under local gauge transformations is proved as a theorem.

covariant phase rate and coherence \[ \Omega_\gamma(t) = \operatorname{Im}\big\langle \hat\psi,\, D_t\hat\psi \big\rangle, \qquad C^{\nabla}_{ij} = \operatorname{Re}\big\langle \hat\psi_i,\ P^{\nabla}_{j\to i}\hat\psi_j \big\rangle \]

There is a price. Parallel transport depends on the path, so a convention must be fixed — the shortest geodesic, the path induced by textual order, or a learned reference path. That is one more implementation decision than the earlier paper required.

② From a line to an arbitrary graph (Wave → Quantum)

WAVEPHASENET

The DFT assumes tokens sit evenly spaced along a single line.

Relations that are not sequential — dependency, coreference, retrieved evidence — have no place in that framework.

→
QUANTUMPHASENET

Replacing it with low eigenmodes of a graph Laplacian performs the same low-frequency extraction over any connectivity structure.

The edge set can mix causal neighborhoods, k-nearest neighbors, syntactic dependency, coreference and retrieval-evidence edges.

A document is not really a line of tokens; it is a network of references. This generalization carries straight into the third paper, where it reappears as a directed graph over tokens, sentences or propositions.

③ From continuous to finite (Quantum → Modular)

QUANTUMPHASENET

Phase lives in a continuous complex amplitude \(\psi=Ae^{i\phi}u\), and spectral selection uses the QFT, phase estimation and amplitude amplification.

Expressive, but it needs complex arithmetic — and consistency can only ever be approximated numerically.

→
MODULARPHASENET

Phase is rounded onto \(q\) ticks and held as a group element \(g^{a}\bmod p\). Composition becomes group multiplication; relative phase becomes group division.

Consistency then reduces to an exact integer comparison, and the same input always yields the same verdict.

covariant residual and cycle holonomy, finite-group version \[ r_{ij}=z_{i}^{-1}U_{ij}z_{j}\in G, \qquad W_{C}=\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}U_{i_{t}i_{t+1}}\bmod p \]

What matters here is that the move buys no additional representational power. The paper says so itself: the modular-exponential representation is isomorphic to the exponent-residue one and creates no new capacity. What it buys instead is exact composition, reproducibility, subgroup projection, and checkability as a checksum. The costs are discretization error (aliasing and code collisions at small \(q\)) and dependence on the choice of generator.

⚠ The third paper is not faster

Falling back to integer arithmetic does not improve the complexity of attention. The \(O(N^2 d)\) of dense attention remains, and the claim is only that the added pair-bias term stays of lower order when \(H\ll d\). The value of the third paper is not acceleration but an inductive bias of exactness and checkability.

05 // CONTINUITY

What carried over, and what did not

No successor subsumes its predecessor. Across the three papers there are claims that disappeared, claims that returned in a different form, and claims that were deliberately dropped.

① Cohomology and Hodge decomposition — partly returned in another form

The first paper's core — window coverings, cochains, coboundaries and semantic gluing through harmonic projection — all but disappeared in the second, where measuring local disagreement moved to curvature and geometric risk.

In the third paper this thread returns as cycle holonomy and discrete integrability. The theorem "if \(W_C=1\) on every cycle, a global assignment of semantic phases exists" is positioned by the paper itself as the finite-cyclic-group counterpart of the second paper's connection, curvature and cohomological consistency.

It is not a full restoration, though, because the objects measured differ. A coboundary measures disagreement between overlapping windows; holonomy measures how far phase fails to return after traversing a closed contextual loop. Whether the latter subsumes the former is still not obvious.

② The 3,000-dimension floor — absent from both successors

Dimensionality reduction via cumulative energy analysis was called the "core contribution" of the first paper, yet neither successor mentions it. The complexity discussion moved to sparse graphs and Chebyshev approximation in the second, and to table lookups and local windows in the third.

Note also that the original figure (82–88% cumulative energy) is a derivation from the 1/f model, not a measurement. Since it can be checked by analyzing the embeddings of an existing model, it remains the cheapest claim to test anywhere in the series.

③ The quantum algorithms — deliberately dropped

The QFT, phase estimation, the Oracle and amplitude amplification that sat at the center of the second paper are all removed in the third. This is a decision, not an oversight: in the second paper's validation, RQ5 (quantum advantage) became a falsification candidate, with the simulated quantum circuit losing to a classical approximation on total cost.

The third paper also drops quant-ph from its submission categories, declaring itself a classical finite-group version that requires no quantum hardware. The quantum component of the second paper was not inherited by the third; it was replaced.

⚠ Reading all three together

With three papers published side by side, a reader cannot easily tell whether each successor replaces its predecessor or complements it. The accurate reading today is that each successor rebuilds part of the earlier work on different mathematics — not all of it.

For cohomology regularization and the dimensionality claim, the first paper remains the reference; for quantum spectral selection and measured results, the second. Reading only the third does not give the whole picture.
