JA EN
HodgePhaseNet in three minutes

Seeing the "flow of meaning" inside an AI
as three components.

HodgePhaseNet is a framework that cleanly splits the "direction of meaning" flowing between sentences, or between claims, into three parts: a consistent direction, small loops and large round trips. The split is fixed mathematically in exactly one way, and the result is fed back into how the AI allocates its attention. The tools built in the three earlier papers serve as raw material for constructing this "flow".

Kiyotaka Kasubuchi & Kazuo Fukiya / September 2026 / Theory and design (experiments still to come)
SCROLL
01 // ISSUE

Knowing not just that something is off, but where

The previous paper, ModularPhaseNet, judged whether things add up after going once around a set of links, with a yes or no. In practice, the next question follows at once: is the mismatch in the overall direction, in a small three-way relation, or in a large loop of the argument?

➡️

Overall direction

The part that can be lined up on a single scale, as in "A is broader than B" and "B is broader than C"

🔺

Small triangular loops

The part where the direction goes all the way around among three items, A→B→C→A. Sometimes broken transitivity, sometimes a legitimate cross-reference

⭕

Large round trips

The part that no small triangle explains, yet keeps circling a long loop of the discussion. Sometimes unsupported circular reasoning, sometimes an intended return to an earlier topic

These three show up mixed together. HodgePhaseNet aims to split the mixed flow into three parts, in exactly one way. Which of the parts is an "error", however, is not decided by the split alone. That is something to test by experiment.

02 // IDEA

Like splitting a river's flow into three

01
NODES, LINKS, FACES

Draw the links of meaning as a "net"

Sentences or claims become points, and the relations between them become arrows, forming a net. On top of that, any triangle whose three relations are judged to compose as a unit gets a membrane stretched across it. Which triangles get a membrane is decided by an explicit rule.

Think of a map with towns as points, roads as arrows and blocks as faces. The idea is to be able to tell enclosed blocks apart from large circuits that are not enclosed.
02
ONE WAY TO SPLIT

There is only one way to split the flow into three

The "strength of flow" on each arrow is split into three: the part explained by differences in height (a consistent direction), eddies inside triangles with a membrane (small loops), and flow around large loops with no membrane (large round trips). It is mathematically guaranteed that this split has no overlap and is fixed in exactly one way (discrete Hodge decomposition).

It is like splitting the flow in a waterway into water running downhill, eddies in small ponds, and water circling a lake. The total energy is exactly the sum of the three energies.
03
BACK TO ATTENTION

Feed the result back into "where to look"

The values of the three components are added as small bonuses or penalties to the attention that decides which words the AI refers to. Set the bonus weight to zero and the model becomes exactly the original model again. Which components are used for bonuses or penalties is learned from data.

Picture adding notes to a map, such as "strong eddies here" or "this is part of a large circuit", to shift slightly where the reader's eyes go. The map itself is not redrawn.

There is one important pitfall. If the strength of each arrow is built only from differences between the values at its points, the eddies and round trips are always zero, and splitting becomes pointless. The paper proves this and makes a "relation encoder", which evaluates each arrow's relation on its own terms, mandatory.

03 // HOW

How one piece of text is processed

1
Build the net
Draw a capped number of arrows between neighboring words, words linked by knowledge or evidence, and words strongly tied by the original attention. Stretch membranes only over triangles the rule allows
2
Score the flow on each arrow
The relation encoder evaluates both directions of each arrow and produces a flow whose sign flips when the direction is reversed. Frequency, phase and finite-group features (from the three earlier papers) and retrieved evidence are the inputs
3
Split into three
Solving just two sparse systems of equations gives the consistent direction, the small loops and the large round trips
4
Feed back into attention
From features that take into account "which way it is looking", build a bonus or penalty for each pair of words and add it to ordinary attention
5
No peeking at the future
When generating text, build the net only from what is visible up to that point. Using later words leaks information even with a mask
6
Calibrate the risk
Compute a risk from the component ratios, the previous paper's round-trip check, evidence support and semantic ambiguity, then calibrate it into a probability on separate labeled data
04 // LINEAGE

Where it sits among the four papers

WavePhaseNet
Decomposes meaning by frequency, treating low frequencies as the overall theme and high frequencies as local expression. Used here as scale features
arXiv:2602.14419
QuantumPhaseNet
Introduces a rule (a connection) for comparing phases independently of coordinates. Used here as comparison features
arXiv:2608.15820
ModularPhaseNet
Checks exactly, in integer arithmetic, whether going once around brings you back to the start. Used here as consistency features
arXiv:2609.06000
HodgePhaseNet
Splits the "flow of meaning" built from the three papers' features uniquely into a consistent direction, small loops and large round trips, and feeds it back into attention
2026.09 / arXiv:2610.14971

Where the three earlier papers each changed the mathematical stage, this paper builds a place that receives and integrates the outputs of all three. ModularPhaseNet's round-trip check answers "consistent or not"; this paper apportions, in real numbers, "which kind of flow the mismatch lies in". The two are meant to be used side by side, not one in place of the other.

05 // STATUS

How much has been confirmed so far

⚠ TO BE CLEAR UP FRONT
  • This paper is a mathematical and architectural proposal; it reports no performance results on natural language.
  • The synthetic-data validation from the previous paper, QuantumPhaseNet, is not used as evidence for this paper.
  • The paper itself states that one cannot say "small loop = contradiction" or "large round trip = missing knowledge".

PROVEN — established mathematically

  • There is exactly one way to split the flow into three, and the three parts are mutually orthogonal
  • Everything lines up on a single scale only when both the loops and the round trips are zero
  • A flow built only from differences of point values has no loops and no round trips
  • Changing how arrow directions are assigned does not change the attention bonuses or penalties
  • When the assumptions hold, changing the choice of coordinates (the gauge) does not change the result
  • In a small five-vertex example, every number checks out exactly

NOT YET — to be tested

  • Whether the loop and round-trip components can tell legitimate cycles from problematic ones
  • Whether it reduces factual errors more than a simple model of the same cost
  • Whether its risk estimates beat existing uncertainty measures
  • Whether conclusions hold when the way membranes are stretched changes
06 // USE CASES

Where it might help in practice

Diagnosing the argument of long documents

Map the relations between claims in a report or specification as a net, and separate whether a mismatch sits in a local three-way relation or in a large loop spanning chapters

→ A lead on what to revisit, beyond "there is a contradiction"

Checking the hierarchy of terms and concepts

Order the hierarchy using the consistent-direction component, and present the loops that cannot be ordered as candidates

→ Surfaces candidate breaks in the hierarchy

Auditing multi-step reasoning

Check numerically whether the chain from evidence to conclusion contains a large loop that no piece of evidence supports

→ Makes candidate circular reasoning visible

A gate on RAG answers

Calibrate a risk combining the component ratios, the round-trip check and evidence support, and route answers above a threshold to re-retrieval or abstention

→ Suits work where abstaining is better than a wrong answer

All of these are uses still to be tested, not effects backed by measurement today. Note in particular that finding a "loop" or a "round trip" does not necessarily mean an error.

07 // SCOPE

What it can and cannot do

CAN

  • Split the flow of meaning uniquely into direction, small loops and large round trips, and observe them as numbers
  • Feed the result back into attention without breaking the original model (zero weight restores it)
  • Bring the features of the three earlier papers together in one place
  • Compute during generation without using future words

CANNOT

  • Judge a loop or a round trip to be an "error" on that basis alone
  • Reach conclusions that do not depend on how the net is built (which triangles get membranes)
  • Make computation faster. During generation it can actually add work
  • Detect cases where the evidence itself is wrong
08 // ADOPTION

If you try it, go in this order

1
Check the decomposition on synthetic data
First confirm that the three components are recovered correctly on nets with known answers. This checks the implementation; it is not evidence of an effect on language
2
Start with one layer and one head
Insert it only in a chosen layer and head, and first confirm that setting the bonus weight to zero restores the original
3
Fix the membrane rule first
Results change with which triangles become faces. Fix a rule that can be decided without looking at evaluation answers, and also compare what happens when it changes
4
Test orientation, gauge and future leakage
Make automated tests that randomly flipping arrow directions does not change results, and that editing later words does not change earlier outputs
5
Compare against same-cost controls
Compare with a relation model of the same size that does not use the decomposition, and with stronger verifiers, to isolate whether gains come from the decomposition
09 // FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from ModularPhaseNet's round-trip check?

ModularPhaseNet decides exactly, over a finite group, whether going once around returns to the start. HodgePhaseNet apportions a real-valued flow, as energy, among a consistent direction, small loops and large round trips. The first is a yes/no check, the second a breakdown, and the paper recommends evaluating the two side by side.

If a "large round trip" is found, does that mean knowledge is missing?

Not necessarily. A text that deliberately returns to its opening topic also produces a round trip. The paper states that "the harmonic component does not automatically mean missing knowledge", and what it means is something to test by experiment.

Is it more expensive to compute?

Yes. A single decomposition only solves sparse systems of equations, but re-solving each time on "only the visible part" while generating text adds cost. The paper does not claim any speedup and asks for measured reports of wall-clock time and memory.

Can it be added to an existing trained model?

By design, yes. It only adds bonuses or penalties to attention, and setting the weight to zero restores the original model exactly. The relation encoder still has to be trained, though.

Do I need to have read the three earlier papers to use it?

The decomposition itself does not depend on them. Their features are optional inputs to the relation encoder, and the plan is to check by ablation which features actually help.

When will experimental results appear?

They are not in this paper. The next steps listed are synthetic recovery with an auditable reference implementation, comparison against matched baselines, and evaluation on independently labeled legitimate and illegitimate cycles.
